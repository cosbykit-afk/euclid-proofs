## Book summary

Book 6 of the *Elements* (33 propositions, 6.1–6.33, plus corollaries to 6.8, 6.19, 6.20) applies the Eudoxean theory of proportion of Book 5 to **plane geometry: similar figures**. Its characteristic machinery is the equimultiple definition of proportion (Def. 5.5): the book is framed by two direct applications of it — Prop. 1 (triangles/parallelograms of the same height are as their bases) and Prop. 33 (in equal circles, angles are as the arcs they stand on) — and between them it develops: the fundamental similarity theorems (6.2, 6.4–6.8), the constructive geometry of proportion (third/fourth/mean proportional, 6.9–6.13, 6.18, 6.25), the rectangle–proportion equivalence (6.16–6.17), the squared-ratio law for similar figures (6.19–6.23), and the **application of areas** (6.27–6.29: geometric solutions of the quadratics x² − αx + β = 0 and x² + αx − β = 0), culminating in the golden section (6.30, on Def. 6.2 extreme and mean ratio), the generalized Pythagoras (6.31: the figure on the hypotenuse equals the sum of similar figures on the legs), and the angle–arc proportion (6.33). Historically (per the encyclopedia article), this book is the basis of Book X's theory of irrationals and of the tangent-secant proportionality underlying later conic theory. In the TESS-India framing, Book 6 is Euclid's own "shape + space, linked with ratio, deduction, and mathematical proof" book.

**The decisive R Theory fact, verified from the texts.** R Theory does *not* inherit this book. Theorem 4.X.P10 (the **no-Euclid-wholesale theorem**, §4.X.H, manuscript lines 24696–24704) states explicitly: "Revised Book 4 does not accept Euclidean metric geometry wholesale before the Flower construction." The inherited synthetic substrate is *strictly weaker* than Euclid's: P4.1 + P4.1-C + P4.2-L + P4.3 (+ S1–S6 / P4.4 for solids). The Eudoxean equimultiple proportion theory of Book 5 and the entire similarity apparatus of Book 6 (Defs. 6.1–6.2, every similarity criterion, the application of areas) are **not imported, not re-derived, and not used** in R Theory Volume I Books 0–6: a full-text search of rewrite books 0–6 finds zero occurrences of "similar"/"similarity", and zero occurrences of "golden", "Fibonacci", or "extreme and mean" anywhere in the manuscript. Where R Theory needs ratio-of-area content it uses modern replacements (Gram quadratic forms, exterior algebra, the determinant transformation law) built on its own admitted premises — never as deductions from Euclid's Book 6 propositions. Each row below records this honestly.
